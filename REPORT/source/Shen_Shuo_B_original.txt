1 · Working title
A short, specific name for your project.
 ExpertFeel — Distilling Human Expert Intuition into an AI Digital Employee

2 · The problem, and why it matters
Who has this problem, and why is it worth solving? 2–3 sentences. (Rubric 1)
 Experienced professionals develop a “feel” through repeated practice: they recognize
 patterns, identify important signals and make judgments that are difficult to express as
 explicit rules.

 The problem is that this implicit expertise is difficult to transfer and scale. My project asks
 whether AI can distil these hidden patterns from an expert’s historical work and
 reproduce the expert’s judgment on unseen cases, rather than merely imitate their
 writing style.

 This matters because successful distillation could turn individual experience into a
 reusable AI digital employee. The project will also test the limits of this approach:
 whether the system merely appears expert-like, or can actually achieve measurable
 predictive performance.

3 · Who it’s for, and the domain
Your user / audience and the industry (e.g. banking, healthcare, retail, your employer).
 Option A — Expert Content Intelligence

 Primary user: Senior content editor / content strategist.

 Persona: An experienced editor deciding which topics or drafts deserve further
 production effort, relying heavily on personal intuition but unable to systematically
 compare hundreds of historical examples.

 Domain: Digital Media / Content Strategy.

 Desired change: The editor can submit a topic or draft and receive a high-performance
 prediction supported by relevant expert cases, and can use the distilled patterns to
 generate or improve content.

 Option B — Expert Valuation Intelligence

 Primary user: Equity research analyst.

 Persona: A junior or mid-level analyst preparing a company valuation who wants to
 access the judgment patterns of experienced analysts without manually reviewing large
 numbers of historical reports.

4 · Why AI — and which kind? Remember, from class 1 workshops, no one size fits all
Is AI the right tool here (vs a rule or lookup)? If yes, which type — narrow ML, a foundation model, RAG, an agent? (ILO 1)
 I will use a hybrid approach rather than assuming that an LLM alone is sufficient.

 For Option A, the non-AI baseline will be a conventional classifier using observable
 features such as topic, text characteristics, publication time and historical engagement.
 The AI system will add an LLM + RAG + expert-pattern distillation layer to capture less
 explicit patterns such as topic selection, framing, narrative structure, timing and
 contextual signals.

 For Option B, the baseline will be a deterministic valuation model using financial ratios
 and standard valuation multiples. The AI system will use LLM + RAG to retrieve
 comparable historical expert cases and distil the reasoning behind assumptions such as
 growth, margins and valuation multiples.

 I will not use an agent in the first version because the core task does not require a multi-
 step tool loop.

5 · Proposed approach — build vs buy or both!
What will you build (low-code and/or code)? Which layers will you own vs rent, and why? (Rubric 2, ILO 3)
 I will adopt a Buy + Build approach.

 I will rent the foundation model and embedding capability through existing APIs, while
 building the expert-specific data layer, retrieval strategy, prompting,
 prediction/generation logic and evaluation pipeline myself.

 The architecture will be:

           Input → Retrieve Relevant Expert Cases → Distil / Apply
           Expert Patterns → Prediction or Generation →
           Evaluation

 I will first test a simple prompted LLM baseline, then add RAG, and finally add the expert-
 distillation layer. This allows me to measure whether each additional layer actually
 improves performance.

 The first implementation will avoid fine-tuning and complex agents because these add
 cost and failure modes before the value of expert distillation has been established.

 Option A — Time-Machine Evaluation

 I will freeze historical cases at Time T. The model will only receive information available at
 T and will predict whether a piece of content will become high-performing. The actual
 engagement outcome after publication becomes the ground truth.

 I will measure metrics such as Precision, Recall, F1 and probability calibration, and
 compare them against the non-AI baseline.

 I will also test:

           Topic → Expert Pattern Retrieval → Content Generation
           → High-Performance Evaluation

 to determine whether distilled expertise improves generated content, not only
 prediction.

 Option B — Time-Machine Valuation Evaluation

 I will freeze a historical company case at Time T and provide only information available at
 that point. The AI's valuation judgment will then be compared with the historical expert
 judgment and, where appropriate, subsequent market outcomes.

 I will measure valuation error and expert-judgment agreement, while comparing the
 result with the deterministic valuation baseline.

 In both options, the system will have an abstain / “insufficient confidence” mechanism,
 and I will measure both its abstention rate and error rate.

8 · Risks, limitations & responsible use
What could go wrong (incl. silent failure), and who might it affect?
 The main risks are:

          Data leakage: future information may accidentally enter historical cases → use strict time-based
           data freezing.
          Confidently wrong outputs: the system may produce convincing but incorrect expert reasoning →
           confidence thresholds and abstention.
          Correlation mistaken for expertise: the model may learn superficial signals rather than expert
           intuition → compare against baselines and conduct ablation tests.
          Expert data limitations: historical expert work may be insufficient or inaccessible → treat data
           availability as a go/no-go condition.
          Domain-specific risk: financial outputs could be misused as investment advice → if Option B is
           selected, the system will be positioned strictly as analyst decision support.

 The key silent failure is a system that sounds like an expert but does not actually predict
 better than a simpler baseline. The evaluation therefore focuses on unseen cases rather
 than stylistic similarity alone.
